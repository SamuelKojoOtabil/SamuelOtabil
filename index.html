Casa Cripina – Accra, Ghana

Project Type: Residential
Location: Accra, Ghana

Description

Casa Cripina was a unique challenge, beginning with an existing ground-floor structure built without architectural consultation or a comprehensive plan. The owner envisioned transforming the building into a modern apartment complex, with provisions for further development already made during the initial construction. This scenario highlights a common issue architects face in Ghana—structures often lack professional input at the early stages, leading to design inefficiencies and limitations.

The project’s primary objective was to give the building a modern facelift while designing two two-bedroom apartments on each floor. The design incorporated open-plan living areas, combining the living room, dining area, and kitchen into cohesive, flowing spaces that maximize usability and comfort.

To enhance the interiors, a modern material palette was employed, blending durability and aesthetic appeal. Large openings were strategically placed to flood the apartments with natural light, creating bright and inviting living spaces that minimize the reliance on artificial lighting during the day.

Key Features

  • Open-Plan Design: Seamlessly integrated living, dining, and kitchen areas to promote functionality and a sense of spaciousness.
  • Natural Lighting: Generous openings designed to maximize natural light and ventilation.
  • Modern Facelift: A fresh material palette brought a contemporary aesthetic to the building, aligning with the client's vision for a modernized structure.

Role

  • Architectural Designer: Redesigned the structure to create efficient layouts for the apartments while improving the overall aesthetic of the building.
  • Project Advisor: Guided the client on sustainable and cost-effective material choices to achieve a modern yet budget-friendly outcome.

Conclusion

Casa Cripina exemplifies the transformative power of architectural intervention, showcasing how thoughtful design can adapt an unplanned structure into a functional and modern apartment complex. The project not only met the client’s vision but also addressed broader challenges in the Ghanaian construction landscape by emphasizing the importance of professional architectural involvement from the outset.

Shama Family House (Net House)

Project Type: Residential (Semi Mixed-Use)
Location: Shama, Ghana

Description

The Shama Family House, also known as the “Net House,” was designed on a historic site that once housed the Boadi family’s old residence. Located between Fort San Sebastián and the Gulf of Guinea, the original structure comprised five small bedrooms shared by nuclear and extended family members.

The client’s primary request for the new design was a large space where family members could convene for discussions, gatherings, and wake-keeping ceremonies. This need shaped the project into a semi mixed-use building that seamlessly blends traditional family values with contemporary functionality.

The ground floor features a versatile 60-seater family meeting hall, which can be used for private gatherings or rented out for public events. Adjacent to this is a three-bedroom flat designed for comfort and privacy. The upper floor accommodates six en-suite bedrooms, a spacious living area, and a sitting area, all oriented to maximize the stunning views of Fort San Sebastián and the bustling coastline of Shama.

A key design element is the use of breeze blocks, referred to as the "net." These blocks screen the bedrooms from external viewers while allowing uninterrupted views of the surroundings and enhancing ventilation. This thoughtful incorporation of traditional elements preserves the essence of communal living while meeting the evolving needs of both younger and older generations.

Key Features

  • Family Meeting Hall: A 60-seater multifunctional space for private family events and rentable gatherings.
  • Integration of Breeze Blocks: Provides privacy and ventilation while maintaining visual connectivity with the surroundings.
  • Functional Layout: Three-bedroom flat on the ground floor, six en-suite bedrooms, and communal living spaces on the upper floor.
  • Views and Orientation: Openings on the north and south strategically frame views of Fort San Sebastián and the coastline.

Role

  • Lead Designer: Created the architectural concept and developed layouts that balanced traditional living with modern requirements.
  • Consultant: Worked closely with the client to align the design with the family’s cultural and functional needs.

Conclusion

The Shama Family House celebrates the richness of Ghanaian traditional living while addressing the practical needs of a large, multigenerational family. Through its thoughtful design and integration of cultural elements like the "net," the building serves as both a functional home and a legacy for the family, embodying their shared history and future aspirations.
